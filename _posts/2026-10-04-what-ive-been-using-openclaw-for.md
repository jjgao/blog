Every time I see another personal-assistant feature announced by one of the big AI companies, I find myself thinking about the assistant I’ve actually been using: OpenClaw. Those launches got me wondering what I’ve come to rely on it for in everyday life.

The answer is a mix of small, practical things. I’ve used OpenClaw to capture links and turn research into notes I can find later; organize college tours and application planning; plan a summer vacation; keep track of World Cup picks and scores; and troubleshoot messaging channels. It’s also helped with work planning and GitHub projects. And, less grandly, I’ve even used it to order coffee through Terminal.shop.

What connects those examples is that they don’t have to end when the conversation does. A chat can turn into a saved note, a plan, a score sheet, or a change in a project—and I can pick it up again later. The assistant is useful not just because it can answer, but because it can help carry things forward across the tools and parts of life I already have.

I suspect that’s where personal assistants are headed. The interesting question isn’t only which one can have the best conversation, but which ones can become a natural, dependable part of the way people actually get things done. I’m curious to see what that looks like as these tools keep evolving.
